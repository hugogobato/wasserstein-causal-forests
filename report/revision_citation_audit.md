## Conceptual corrections that affect citations and prose

The propensity score is the scalar function
\(e(x)=\Pr(A=1\mid X=x)\), not a synonym for endogenous adoption. A nonconstant propensity score, or a score different from one half, only says that assignment probabilities vary with measured covariates. Endogenous or confounded adoption concerns association between treatment and potential outcomes, either through observed prognostic variables that are not adjusted for or through unmeasured variables. In the present simulation, the adoption index depends on covariates that also enter the potential-outcome law. The clean paper-level description is therefore “covariate-dependent treatment assignment aligned with prognostic covariates” or “selection on observed prognostic covariates under the stated unconfoundedness design.” If the intended claim is unobserved endogeneity, the current DGP does not establish it.

The fact that \(e(x)\) is not one half is not by itself the reason to expect a method to fail. The relevant simulation feature is that \(e(x)\) changes across the covariate support and those same coordinates affect the conditional law. A propensity model may be logistic for the experiment, but the method description should state that any suitable binary classification learner could be used, with clipping or other positivity safeguards chosen as implementation details.

The displayed augmented inverse-propensity score is a standard doubly robust score for a scalar functional \(h(q)\), under consistency, conditional exchangeability, positivity, and suitable nuisance convergence. Cite Bang and Robins (2005), with Chernozhukov et al. (2018) as a modern cross-fitting reference. The current argument supports doubly robust calibration of the reported functionals. It does not establish double robustness of the entire particle law, of the tree partition, or of the finite-sample WCF predictor. It also does not support an efficiency claim unless an influence-function calculation and regularity conditions are supplied. A safe sentence is: “The calibration step uses the standard augmented inverse-propensity score, so the corresponding functional estimand has the usual double-robust identification property when either the propensity nuisance or the conditional functional regression is consistently estimated.”

The paper should not say that WCF is “the first” doubly robust distributional method without a scoped, exhaustive novelty review. The 2023 kernel-treatment-effect test, Kallus and Oprescu's 2023 conditional distributional treatment-effect procedure, and Jain and Luedtke's 2026 preprint are direct prior art. A defensible contribution statement is that WCF combines a Wasserstein-quantile particle representation, a shared treatment-arm partition, and standard doubly robust functional calibration in one distribution-valued learner. Any novelty claim should be limited to that combination and the exact estimands.

For strict propriety, replace the current sole citation to Székely and Rizzo with Gneiting and Raftery (2007). The finite-particle, collision-smoothed objective is a restricted computational score. The paper can say that the unsmoothed energy score is strictly proper under its moment conditions and that the implementation minimizes a smoothed finite-particle approximation. It should not imply that the finite \(M\), smoothed optimization has inherited the full population theorem automatically.

The two-part extension has a standard motivation independent of the uncertain applied study. Health-care expenditures are nonnegative with a substantial exact-zero component and positive continuous expenditures, as modeled in Duan et al. (1983). Modified count models and excess-zero mechanisms are classic in econometrics, as in Mullahy (1986). These references support a paragraph saying that a point mass plus a positive-part law is useful whenever “participation” and “intensity conditional on participation” are distinct data-generating mechanisms. For the present distribution-valued design, the point mass should be described as a degenerate outcome law, such as a zero quantile vector, rather than as a generic scalar zero. The WCF implementation is an optional extension for such outcomes, not evidence that the eventual application must be a health or benefit dataset.

## Recommended claim map for the revision

The introduction should cite Park et al. (2021), Muandet et al. (2021), Kallus and Oprescu (2023), and Näf et al. (2026) for the existing distributional treatment-effect landscape. It should then state the gap narrowly: existing methods represent conditional laws or kernel contrasts, whereas this paper evaluates a Wasserstein-quantile particle learner with shared treatment-arm geometry and functional calibration under covariate-dependent assignment.

The method section should cite Meinshausen (2006) and Cevid et al. (2022) only when describing forest-law baselines, Gneiting and Raftery (2007) for the energy score, Bang and Robins (2005) for the AIPW property, and Rosenbaum and Rubin (1983) when defining the propensity score. The reference-adjusted TATE and TCATE are the paper's estimands; they need precise definitions and an explanation of their interpretation, but no citation is required for the authors' own contrast.

The result section should report the final WCF estimator under one name. Internal names such as WCF-v1, WCF-R3, and repair iterations should be removed. PTA-S should be omitted if it is no longer a complete law estimator. Simulation regimes may use an income-distribution example to make the equations concrete, but the prose should describe the DGP first and list income, wage, expenditure, or benefit distributions only as possible domains in which such an object could arise.

The result narrative should say that the proposed learner performs well in the tested regimes with covariate-dependent propensities and uneven treatment-arm support. It should not generalize that non-half propensities alone cause failure or success. The observed ablation can motivate a restrained statement that the ranking changes when the assignment mechanism is made independent of the prognostic covariates, subject to the exact metric and DGP used, without carrying the discarded skewness and scale hypotheses into the final paper.

Finally, remove “preregistered simulation program,” “frozen manifests,” “repair iterations,” “registered gate,” “not a theorem,” and “internal” language from the main narrative. A normal paper can state the simulation design, seeds, implementation choices, and reproducibility materials in Methods or a supplement. The contribution should be stated as a result of the completed study, not as a history of how the project was debugged.
