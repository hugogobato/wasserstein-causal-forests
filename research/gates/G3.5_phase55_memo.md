## 2. `cwdb_rmean`: the vector R-learner passes its mechanism screen

The R-learner's hypothesis was that the weak common-target performance is
confounding and prognostic leakage rather than the particle representation.
The screen asked for a credible D2/D8 contrast improvement, with D8 as the
primary confounding test, D2 held to the inherited null cap, and D0 checked
for material bias.

| Check | n = 500 | n = 1000 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| D0 `mean_quantile_rmse` | 0.1095 | 0.0842 | under the 0.15 cap |
| D2 `mean_quantile_rmse` | 0.0149 | 0.0233 | under the 0.15 cap |
| D2 false-effect ratio vs best frozen baseline at that n | 0.24 (baseline 0.0620) | 0.48 (baseline 0.0482) | under 1.25 |
| D8 vs PTA-S, paired difference | −0.0168 (SE 0.0047) | −0.0032 (SE 0.0047) | win at n = 500, direction at n = 1000 |
| D2 vs PTA-S, paired difference | −0.0471 (SE 0.0049) | −0.0249 (SE 0.0044) | win, 4 to 5 SE |
| D2 vs R3, paired difference | −0.0631 (SE 0.0228) | −0.0178 (SE 0.0065) | win, 2.8 and 2.7 SE |
| D0 vs PTA-S, paired difference | **+0.0472 (SE 0.0047)** | **+0.0377 (SE 0.0017)** | **loss, 10 and 22 SE** |
| D0 vs R3, paired difference | +0.0078 (SE 0.0056) | −0.0026 (SE 0.0032) | tie |

The false-effect ratio is the script's convention (`research/checks/
phase55_stage1_report.py`, `rule1_block`): each sample size's D2 mean over the
best frozen baseline *at that same sample size*. An earlier draft of this memo
divided both sample sizes by a hand-pooled baseline of 0.0551 and reported
0.27 and 0.42. Both conventions clear the 1.25 cap and the verdict is
unchanged, but only one convention is in the analysis code, and it is this one.

The D8 improvement over PTA-S is the phase's target: PTA-S is the method that
beats R3 on five of the seven grid-mean comparisons, and on the primary
confounding regime the R-learner is more accurate at n = 500 by 3.6 paired
standard errors (0.0937 against 0.1105). At n = 1000 the same comparison is
directionally in rmean's favour but does not clear two standard errors.

The honest negative is the R3 comparison on D8: rmean loses by 0.0094
(SE 0.0041) at n = 500 and 0.0197 (SE 0.0018) at n = 1000. The particle
booster's gradient targets average over M = 10 particles before any leaf fit,
and that averaging is variance reduction a mean-only residualised learner does
not have. The claim made here is therefore the one the phase asked for: the
orthogonalization fixes the weak comparison against the direct learner, not a
free lunch against the repaired particle claimant.

The selection behaves as the repair memo taught: held-out R-loss picks
lambda = 0 on D0 (median), 500 on D2 and D8, and 50 on D7, so the adaptivity
that repaired rule 1 in the particle world transfers to the R-loss world, and
the D0 material-bias check passes because the ridge switches itself off where
the data are deterministic.

**The second honest negative is D0 against PTA-S**, added to the table above
because the WP5.5-B numerical hook asks for the D0 paired difference against
both comparators and an earlier draft reported only that D0 sat under the
0.15 cap. rmean loses D0 to PTA-S by 10 paired standard errors at n = 500 and
22 at n = 1000, a far more decisive margin than either of its wins. The margin
is not rmean's alone: R3 loses the same comparison by a similar amount
(0.1017 and 0.0868 against 0.0465), and rmean ties R3 on D0 at both sample
sizes, so this is the whole C-WDB family's deterministic-regime deficit rather
than a cost of orthogonalization. That framing is an explanation, not a
dismissal: rmean is being proposed as the fix for the weak PTA-S comparison,
and on one of the four screened regimes the weak PTA-S comparison is not
fixed. Stage 2 must report D0 alongside D2 and D8 rather than as a cap check.

## 3. `cwdb_mutau`: the particle mu/tau variant passes with flags

The screen asked whether the propensity-centred treatment basis retains
particle validity and D7 transfer. The unit tests pin the construction: with
the leaf-share basis and zero penalty the tree and the whole booster reduce
exactly (1e-14) to the reparameterized shared tree, the contrast field
collapses on the null regime while the pooled law stays stable, and the
zero-contrast limit is a treatment-invariant pooled booster.

| Check | n = 500 | n = 1000 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| D0 `mean_quantile_rmse` | 0.1063 | 0.0907 | under the 0.15 cap |
| D2 `mean_quantile_rmse` | 0.0714 | 0.0349 | under the 0.15 cap |
| D2 false-effect ratio vs best frozen baseline at that n | 1.15 | 0.72 | under 1.25 |
| D8 vs PTA-S, paired difference | −0.0261 (SE 0.0055) | −0.0118 (SE 0.0056) | win at both n, 4.8 and 2.1 SE |
| D8 vs R3, paired difference | 0.0000 (SE 0.0031) | +0.0110 (SE 0.0018) | tie, then 6 SE loss |
| D0 vs R3, paired difference | **+0.0045 (SE 0.0021)** | **+0.0039 (SE 0.0016)** | **loss, 2.2 and 2.4 SE** |
| D0 vs PTA-S, paired difference | +0.0440 (SE 0.0013) | +0.0442 (SE 0.0020) | loss, 33 and 22 SE |
| D2 vs PTA-S, paired difference | +0.0094 (SE 0.0149) | −0.0133 (SE 0.0069) | tie, then direction |
| D7 mean-q vs R3 | −0.0043 (SE 0.0034) | +0.0013 (SE 0.0040) | tie |
| D7 `kernel_law_error` vs R3 | −0.0001 | +0.0001 | tie |
| effective support (M = 10), on D7 and D8 | 10.00 (D7) | 10.00 (D8) | no collapse |
| median runtime vs Causal-DRF | | 91x measured | flag, see below |

The columns of the effective-support row are the two regimes Stage 1 scored it
on, not two sample sizes; D6 mode coverage arrives at Stage 2.

On the mean-quantile target the mu/tau variant is close to R3 but not
interchangeable with it: it loses D0 by 2.2 and 2.4 paired standard errors and
D8 at n = 1000 by 6.1, and wins nothing against R3 above two standard errors.
Read as a whole, the propensity-centred basis buys the PTA-S comparison and
pays R3 a small, consistent tax. Two consequences are worth stating. It wins
D8 against
PTA-S by more than two paired standard errors at both sample sizes, which is
an accuracy win on a target PTA-S itself estimates, the kind of win rule 4
had to make from capability alone for R3. And its D2 false-effect behaviour is
driven by the propensity-centred basis plus the same held-out energy-risk
selection, which again picks lambda = 0 on D0 and large strengths on D2 and
D8, matching the R3 pattern.

**Flag 1, D7 shape transfer at n = 500.** On `TATE-K-grid_skewness` and
`TCATE-K-grid_skewness` at n = 500, mutau is worse than R3 by 0.058 (SE 0.022)
and 0.062 (SE 0.024), about 2.5 paired standard errors. At n = 1000 the same
comparisons are directionally better (−0.003, −0.007), and the upper-tail
functional and the law metric are ties at both sample sizes. The phase
document forbids a candidate that improves D2 by materially degrading D7 shape
transfer; this is not material degradation at the working resolution, but the
n = 500 asymmetry is exactly the kind of small-sample shape loss the D7 screen
exists to catch, and it must be re-checked at Stage 2 rather than assumed away.

**Flag 2, cost.** The measured median runtime ratio is 91 times Causal-DRF,
against the inherited cap of 60. The structure explains most of it: stable
selection costs three folds over three candidates plus the final fit, about
ten ordinary fits per cell, against R3's six. The measurement itself was
inflated by a heavily loaded machine (load average above 15 during the run),
and the per-cell cost before load was estimated near the cap, but the honest
statement is that mutau as configured does not clearly clear rule 6. Stage 2
must either reduce the selection cost or declare the justification for the
cap, and the memo proposes the reduction first: the three-fold scan exists to
make the null-regime choice stable, and a stable two-fold scan over the same
candidates would buy back roughly a third of the cost if the D2 flip it
produced on one pilot seed can be demonstrated to be harmless at Stage 2.

## 4. `cwdb_xmean`: the vector X-learner fails its mechanism screen

The X-learner's intended advantage was to appear under imbalance or overlap
stress and nowhere else. It does not.

On the imbalance suite, against rmean on the same cells: D7-imb is a clear win
(−0.0456, SE 0.0046), but D2-imb is a clear loss (+0.0553, SE 0.0069, about
8 paired standard errors) and D8-imb is a marginal loss (+0.0121, SE 0.0064).
D2-imb is the null regime with a minority treated arm, and the X-learner
manufactures contrast noise there, which is precisely the failure mode the
phase exists to avoid. On the balanced main grid it neither wins nor shows a
credible mechanism: no 2-SE win against PTA-S or R3 on any regime except a
single D2 n = 1000 comparison, and it is the only Phase 5.5 variant that
breaks an inherited correctness cap outright, failing the D0 cap of 0.15 with
0.3545 and 0.3536 at n = 500 and n = 1000.

The imputation device itself is verified by the unit tests: no unit helps
create its own pseudo-outcome, and with exact nuisances both pseudo-outcome
conditional means are the contrast.

**Why it fails, stated so it can be falsified.** An earlier draft attributed
the failure to the doubled pseudo-outcome variance. That explanation is wrong,
and the D0 numbers refute it without any new run: 0.3545 at n = 500 and 0.3536
at n = 1000 is flat in n, and a variance penalty shrinks with n. The D0 error
is a bias floor produced by the frozen three-step effect budget, whose total
boosting weight is 3 x 0.12 = 0.36 against the R-loss contrast's 2.4, so the
effect regression barely leaves its intercept. `research/checks/
phase55_xmean_budget_probe.py` sweeps the budget over {3, 10, 20, 50} and
records the result in `results/merged_phase55/xmean_budget_probe.json`:

| Regime, n = 500 | 3 (frozen) | 10 | 20 | 50 | `cwdb_rmean` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D0 | 0.353 | 0.167 | 0.085 | 0.066 | |
| D2 | 0.056 | 0.096 | 0.125 | 0.167 | |
| D8 | 0.104 | 0.145 | 0.175 | 0.221 | |
| D2-imb | 0.068 | 0.113 | 0.150 | 0.193 | 0.009 |
| D7-imb | 0.082 | 0.118 | 0.148 | 0.189 | 0.131 |
| D8-imb | 0.102 | 0.153 | 0.190 | 0.234 | 0.093 |

This makes the negative finding stronger, not weaker, and moves it off the
budget. First, the frozen budget was not miscalibrated: it is the best of the
four on all three imbalance regimes, the regimes that decide the verdict, and
even at its own optimum the X-learner still loses D2-imb and D8-imb to rmean.
Second, no budget serves both ends of the sweep, since the setting that makes
D0 recoverable is the setting that destroys the null and confounded regimes.
The honest statement is therefore that the vector X pseudo-outcome regression
has no single complexity that is simultaneously effect-recovering and
null-safe, and that unlike rmean it has no adaptive regularizer to choose one
per regime: rmean's ridge is selected per cell on held-out R-loss and picks
lambda = 0 on D0 and 500 on D2, while xmean's only complexity control is the
globally frozen step count. If the X mechanism is ever revisited, the change
that would make it a fair comparison is a cross-fitted per-cell selection over
its effect budget, not a different frozen constant.

Per the decision table, the X-learner is not retained as a general variant;
its rows stay in the merged table and the mechanism result is reported as a
negative finding.

## 6. Stage 2 plan and declared risks

Stage 2 runs the frozen comparison on the inherited G3 coordinates for
`cwdb_rmean` (common mean-contrast targets only) and `cwdb_mutau` (all law and
functional targets), against PTA-S, Causal-DRF, W-DRF-T, and R3, with the
inherited correctness, law, transfer, no-collapse, and cost thresholds. The
following are declared before Stage 2 rather than discovered in it.

1. The D7 n = 500 skewness transfer flag must be re-measured on the full 20-seed manifest; a 2-SE degradation there disqualifies mutau from the full-law claim under the phase's own rule.
2. The cost cap: mutau must either be shown inside 60 times Causal-DRF at Stage 2 load, or the selection cost must be reduced (the two-fold scan with its one known D2 pilot flip) and the D2 stability re-verified at 20 seeds.
3. The regimes Stage 1 does not touch arrive at Stage 2: D3 sharing against `cwdb_v0`, D5 law separation, D6 mode coverage and the squared-W2 repulsion ablation, and D9 overlap deterioration.
4. `cwdb_rmean` and `cwdb_xmean` are mean-only by contract: no law metric may be requested of them, and none was.
5. D0 is a declared open weakness for both surviving variants, not a passed cap. Both lose it to PTA-S decisively (rmean by 10 and 22 paired SE, mutau by 33 and 22), and mutau also loses D0 to R3 by 2.2 and 2.4 SE. Stage 2 reports the D0 paired differences against both comparators in the main table. If mutau's D0-versus-R3 tax persists at 20 seeds alongside its D8 n = 1000 loss, the case for replacing R3 rests on the PTA-S comparison alone and the memo should say so rather than claim parity.
6. `arm_shrinkage = 5.0` appears in the frozen Stage 1 manifest for `cwdb_mutau` and is inert: `MutauSharedTreeRegressor` overrides `_leaf_values`, the only consumer of that knob, so `contrast_shrinkage` was the sole live regularizer. Nothing in the Stage 1 results changes, and `tests/test_cwdb_mutau.py::test_the_inherited_arm_shrinkage_knob_is_inert` now pins the fact. The Stage 2 manifest must drop the key rather than record a strength that never applied.
7. The two selection procedures are not equally nested and their costs are not comparable as measured. `MutauCWDBRegressor` refits everything, propensity included, inside every selection fold; `VectorRLearner` reuses one full-sample out-of-fold nuisance stack across its selection folds, which leaves a second-order path from a selection holdout's outcome into the training residuals of the model that scores it. Stage 2 either nests rmean's selection fully or declares the approximation where the runtime ratio is reported.
